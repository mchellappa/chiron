# Rubric: Unit B0 - Orientation

Core claim: in an Agentic SDLC, a ticket's label is the entire
coordination mechanism (choreography, not orchestration) - knowing what
state a ticket is in tells you, and every agent, whose turn it is.

## Pass
- Correctly names two plausible next states from `stage:design-ready`
  (e.g. `stage:design-await` and `stage:build-ready`) and explains what
  would cause each.
- States clearly that the label itself - not a conversation or status
  meeting - is what tells every agent/human what's happening.

**Chiron's action:** Affirm the two correct paths and the reasoning.
Ask a forward question bridging to B1: "what do you think happens if
something tries to skip from `requirements-ready` straight to
`build-ready`?" and see if they predict the rejection correctly before
moving on.

## Developing
- Names one plausible next state correctly but can't articulate the
  second, or confuses who/what is responsible for the transition (e.g.
  thinks a human must manually trigger every step rather than an agent
  reacting to the label).
- Understands "labels matter" in the abstract but can't explain why this
  differs from a status meeting or ticket comment.

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Walk through the Unit B0 diagram again
step by step, having them narrate out loud what triggers each transition,
before re-asking.

## Not Yet
- Assumes a central controller/human manually directs every stage (the
  orchestration model this unit explicitly contrasts with choreography).
- Cannot name any plausible next state from the given ticket label.

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Re-explain using the dance vs.
orchestra analogy from Unit B1 early (even though it's formally introduced
there, pulling it forward here often unlocks the concept), then retry the
self-check.
