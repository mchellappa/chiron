# Rubric: Capstone - One Ticket, Start to Finish

Core claim: the eight steps (A1 diagnosis -> A2 gap-closing -> A3
three-question test -> B2 agent-ready ticket + sub-tasks -> B1/B3 sandbox
execution -> B4 cutover plan -> A4 outcome sentence) must function as one
connected chain on a single scenario, not as eight isolated answers.

This rubric is holistic, not per-step - the Capstone tests whether the
chain holds together, not whether each step independently passes.

## Pass
- Produces real artifacts (not descriptions of what they'd write) for
  every step: an actual diagnosis with evidence, an actual ticket text, an
  actual sandbox export or described log, an actual outcome-check
  sentence.
- Later steps are consistent with earlier ones (e.g. the ticket's scope
  matches the diagnosis from step 1; the outcome-check sentence in step 8
  actually measures what step 4's acceptance criteria promised).
- Can explain, when asked, how a decision in one step shaped a later step
  (e.g. "because I diagnosed this as vision-doesn't-exist, my ticket in
  step 4 included a discovery sub-task before any build sub-task").

**Chiron's action:** Affirm the specific connective tissue you observed
between steps - name it explicitly, since that's the actual skill being
tested. This is program completion; reflect back the overall arc of
growth across Track A and B rather than just this one exercise.

## Developing
- All eight artifacts exist, but one or two are inconsistent with each
  other (common failure: the outcome-check sentence in step 8 measures
  something different from what the acceptance criteria in step 4
  promised).
- Chain mostly holds, but the learner jumped ahead at some point (e.g.
  wrote ticket/code before fully diagnosing ambiguity) and back-filled the
  earlier steps to match afterward rather than letting them genuinely
  drive the later ones.

**Chiron's action:** Do not treat as complete. Point at the specific
inconsistency between two named steps and ask them to resolve it by
revising forward from the earlier step, not by patching the later one.

## Not Yet
- Steps exist as disconnected answers - each individually reasonable, but
  with no evidence the earlier steps actually informed the later ones.
- One or more steps are missing entirely, or are descriptions ("I would
  write a ticket that...") rather than real artifacts.

**Chiron's action:** Do not treat as complete. Identify which Track A/B
units correspond to the weakest steps and suggest revisiting those units'
rubrics before reattempting the Capstone as a connected whole.
