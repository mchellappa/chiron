# Rubric: Unit A3 - Anti-Tunnel-Vision Solution Judgment

Core claim: before committing effort, apply three questions in order -
what decision does this unblock, what's the cheapest evidence, and only
then what's the smallest code - to catch the reflex of reaching for a
familiar tool before confirming it's the right-sized response.

## Pass
- Walks through a real example of starting to build something that turned
  out to be wrong, and correctly identifies at which of the three
  questions the mismatch would have surfaced earlier.
- Shows they understand the questions are ordered (cheapest evidence
  before code), not just a checklist to tick in any order.

**Chiron's action:** Affirm which question caught it and why that
ordering mattered. Ask: "what would question 1 have forced you to name
explicitly, that you skipped past at the time?" Advance if they can
answer concretely.

## Developing
- Identifies a relevant example but applies the three questions loosely
  or out of order (e.g. jumps straight to "what's the smallest code"
  without first establishing what decision it unblocks).
- Understands the concept abstractly but the retroactive analysis of
  their own example is shallow ("I guess I should have thought about it
  more").

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Have them redo the retroactive
analysis question-by-question, in order, out loud, rather than as a
single vague reflection.

## Not Yet
- Cannot connect the three questions to their own experience at all, or
  defends jumping straight to a familiar solution as efficient rather
  than recognizing the risk.
- Treats "MVP thinking" as just "build a smaller version of my planned
  feature" rather than "find the cheapest way to learn if the plan is
  even right" - the exact misconception the unit calls out.

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Use the unit's own worked example
(the recommendation engine) and have them apply the three questions to a
fresh scenario you provide, before returning to their own self-check
example.
