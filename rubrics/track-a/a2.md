# Rubric: Unit A2 - Manufacturing Clarity Through Orchestration

Core claim: in a deep/legacy environment, the smallest useful action is
often "get the right person or agent involved for 15 minutes," not
"personally become competent enough to do it alone." The bottleneck test
("am I about to spend more than an hour becoming competent in something
I'll only need once?") is how you catch this in the moment.

## Pass
- Identifies a real task where they spent real time on narrow, one-off
  competence, and names a *specific* person/agent/document that could have
  closed the gap instead (not a vague "I could have asked someone").
- Names the actual blocker honestly (pride, habit, not knowing who to ask)
  rather than a rationalized excuse ("there was no one available" when
  there plausibly was).

**Chiron's action:** Affirm the honesty specifically - this is the hardest
unit to self-assess honestly, so call that out. Ask a forward question:
"next time you notice that one-hour signal, what's the first concrete
action you'll take?" Advance if the answer is a specific action, not a
platitude.

## Developing
- Gives an example but the "orchestration alternative" they name is still
  vague (e.g. "I could have gotten help") without a specific who/what.
- Identifies the pattern correctly in hindsight but can't yet say what
  blocked them from doing it in the moment.

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Push for specificity: "name the actual
person or system you'd have gone to, by name or role." If they still can't,
that's informative - note it and revisit after Unit A3.

## Not Yet
- Treats "orchestration" as just "delegate the whole task to someone else"
  rather than "close a specific narrow gap, then continue owning the
  outcome yourself."
- Can't produce an example at all, or insists they never fall into this
  trap (overconfidence is itself a signal worth probing gently).

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Walk through the unit's own worked
example (the legacy portal mockup) again, specifically contrasting
"delegate the whole thing" vs. "close one 15-minute gap, then you do the
rest" - the distinction is likely what's missing.
