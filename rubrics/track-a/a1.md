# Rubric: Unit A1 - Why Business Can't Hand You a Clear Ask

Core claim: ambiguity has two distinct causes (vision-doesn't-exist vs.
vision-wasn't-communicated), and the diagnostic question
("if I built the obvious first guess, what would you check first?")
is how you tell them apart before choosing a response.

## Pass
- Correctly classifies their chosen example as one of the two types, with
  reasoning tied to how the stakeholder would plausibly respond to the
  diagnostic question (not just an assertion of which type it is).
- Can state, unprompted, that the two types require different responses
  (co-discovery vs. extraction) - not just that they're "different."

**Chiron's action:** Affirm the reasoning specifically (not just "correct").
Probe one edge case: "what if the stakeholder gives a vague answer at
first but turns out to have a specific number in mind once pressed - which
type was it really?" to test they understand this is a diagnostic, not a
label applied once and forgotten. Advance if they handle the edge case
reasonably.

## Developing
- Picks a type but the reasoning is just a guess dressed as diagnosis
  ("this one feels like Ask 1") without applying the actual diagnostic
  question to their example.
- Understands the two types exist but can't yet explain why the response
  differs (treats co-discovery and extraction as interchangeable).

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Make them literally run the
diagnostic question against their example out loud: "what would the
stakeholder actually say if you asked them that?" Re-ask the self-check
once this is done properly.

## Not Yet
- Conflates the two types, or claims all ambiguity should be resolved the
  same way (e.g. "just ask more questions" for every case, including
  vision-doesn't-exist situations where more questions produce silence).
- Cannot produce a real or plausible example at all - stuck on recall of
  the unit's own worked example instead of applying it.

**Chiron's action:** Do not advance. Return to the unit's "Ask 1 vs Ask 2"
contrast with a new pair of examples the learner hasn't seen, and have them
practice classifying those first before retrying their own self-check
example.
