# Unit A4 - Outcome vs. Output

## Start with two "done"s

Engineer A says: "Done - I shipped the shortened onboarding flow, four
screens instead of eight, deployed Friday."

Engineer B says: "Done - new advisor time-to-first-sale dropped from 12
days to 7 after the shortened flow went live; I checked three weeks of data
before calling it done."

Both engineers may have written the same code. Only one of them knows
whether the work actually mattered.

## The principle

**Output is what you produced. Outcome is whether the thing you produced
changed the number the business actually cares about.** They are not the
same, and shipping output confidently creates a false sense of completion -
"done" starts to mean "I finished my part," which is precisely the
stage-of-a-relay thinking Unit A0 said an FDE refuses.

This is the final check on everything else in this track: Unit A1 helped
you figure out what clarity was missing, Unit A2 and A3 got you to build
the smallest useful thing fast and for the right reason - but none of that
matters if you never go back and check whether it worked.

## Why this is easy to skip

Checking outcome requires going back to something you've already called
"done," at a point when you're already moving on to the next task, and
sometimes confirming that it *didn't* work - which is uncomfortable to
surface, especially if deploying it took real effort. The organizational
incentive (ticket closed, sprint velocity counted) rewards output. Outcome
checking is not automatically rewarded the same way - you have to do it
because the role requires it, not because anyone is explicitly asking.

## A simple habit

Before calling anything "done," write down, in one sentence, **what metric
or decision this was supposed to move, and when you will check it.** Not
"this will probably help" - a specific thing, a specific time. Then
actually check it at that time, even if nobody asks you to, and say so out
loud (to the stakeholder, to your team) whether it worked or not.

If you can't write that one sentence before shipping, that's a signal you
skipped Unit A1's diagnostic step and built something without a clear
target in the first place.

## Worked example

Continuing the onboarding scenario: before deploying the 4-screen flow, the
sentence would be: "This should reduce new-advisor time-to-first-sale,
check at 3 weeks post-launch against the prior 12-day baseline." Three weeks
later, you pull the number. If it dropped to 7 days - genuine outcome,
worth saying so. If it stayed at 12 days, the output shipped, but the
outcome didn't follow - and that's valuable information too: maybe screen
count wasn't the actual bottleneck, and Unit A1's diagnosis needs revisiting
with a new round of co-discovery.

## The failure mode this corrects

This directly answers "don't understand business objectives, outcomes" -
not by teaching an abstract definition, but by making outcome-checking a
concrete habit with a deadline, attached to every piece of work, not a
separate "soft skill" exercise.

> that without new investigation, what does that tell you about how
> "done" was defined at the time?
