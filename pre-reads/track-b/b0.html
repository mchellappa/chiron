Prerequisite: Track A complete. Everything here assumes you already have the instincts from A0-A4 (own the outcome, diagnose ambiguity, orchestrate instead of going solo, test before building, check outcome not output). Track B is about applying those instincts inside a specific delivery mechanism - an agentic pipeline.

Start with what changes, and what doesn't

In Track A, "manufacture clarity" and "orchestrate instead of going solo" were general skills - you could apply them with a whiteboard and a phone call. In an Agentic SDLC, those same instincts now have to work through a specific mechanism: tickets, labels, AI agents doing stages of work, and a system that tracks all of it.

The business ambiguity problem doesn't go away. The orchestration instinct doesn't go away. What's new is the machinery you're orchestrating through - and that machinery has its own rules that, if you don't understand them, will make you look like you're fighting the system instead of using it.

What is an Agentic SDLC

An Agentic SDLC is a software delivery lifecycle where AI agents, not just humans, perform stages of the work - drafting requirements, proposing a design, writing code, running tests, preparing a release - and a shared system of record (tickets) tracks whose turn it is and what state things are in.

The critical design choice in most agentic pipelines, including this one, is choreography over orchestration: there is no single controller telling every agent what to do next. Instead, each agent watches the shared state (a ticket's labels) and reacts when it sees work addressed to its stage - the way dancers follow the music rather than a single conductor calling every movement.

Reading a ticket's lifecycle

SANDBOX-101 -> stage:requirements-ready | v (Requirements agent/human picks it up) stage:requirements-running | v (requirements finished) stage:requirements-done | v (Design stage begins) stage:design-ready | +------+------+ v v stage:design-await stage:build-ready (needs a human (design clear enough, confirm/decision) hand to Build)

Every ticket's label tells you - and every agent watching it - exactly what state it's in and whose turn it is to act. This is the entire coordination mechanism. There is no separate "status meeting" to find out what's happening; the label is the status, and it's visible to every agent and human at once.

Why this matters for an FDE specifically

Recall Track A's orchestration lesson (Unit A2): your job is often "pull in the right thing for 15 minutes," not "do everything yourself." In an Agentic SDLC, "the right thing" is frequently an agent, and "pulling it in" means getting a ticket into the correct label state so the right agent picks it up - not messaging someone directly.

An FDE who doesn't understand the label state machine will try to orchestrate by talking to people, while the actual mechanism for getting an agent's help is invisible to them. Reading the pipeline correctly is itself an orchestration skill, just translated into this specific system.
