Capstone

One Ticket, Start to Finish

FDE Dojo / Capstone

What this is

Every unit so far has isolated one skill: diagnosing ambiguity, orchestrating instead of going solo, reading ticket state, writing agent-ready specs, naming branches correctly, checking outcome instead of output. The Capstone asks you to run all of them together, in order, against a single scenario - the way the job actually works. Nobody hands you "the Unit A1 part" separately from "the Unit B3 part." It all happens on one ticket, at once.

There is no new concept here. This is a test of whether the instincts from Track A and Track B actually operate together, under one continuous thread, not whether you can recite any one of them in isolation.

The scenario

A stakeholder messages you:

"Advisors keep calling support asking why a client's beneficiary update didn't go through. Can you look into it?"

That is the entire starting input. No ticket exists yet. Nobody has measured anything. You do not know if this is a UI problem, a data problem, a process problem, or a training problem. Treat this as a real intake, not a test question with a hidden right answer.

Walk it through every stage

  1. Diagnose the ambiguity. Is this vision-doesn't-exist or vision-wasn't-communicated? Use the diagnostic question. A1
  2. Decide what you personally don't know, and who or what would close that gap fastest - a domain expert, an agent, an existing log/document. Name the specific gap, not just "I'd ask someone." A2
  3. Apply the three-question test before proposing any build: what decision does this unblock, what's the cheapest evidence, and only then, what's the smallest code (if any). A3
  4. Write the agent-ready ticket that would result from the above: acceptance criteria, scope, non-goals. B2
  5. Break it into sub-tasks with correct dependency (Blocks) order, the way a Design stage would. B2
  6. Work the ticket through the sandbox (sandbox.html): transition the label state correctly, and produce a correctly formatted branch name and PR title for at least one sub-task. B1 / B3
  7. Write the cutover plan: verification step, rollback awareness, and the stakeholder message you'd send at deploy. B4
  8. Write the outcome-check sentence: the specific metric or decision this was supposed to move, and when you'll check it. A4

Rules for this exercise

  • Do it in order - don't jump to step 6 because it's the most concrete. The ordering is the point; most real failures come from skipping straight to code.
  • Write actual artifacts, not descriptions of what you'd write. A real ticket text, a real branch name, a real one-sentence outcome check - not "I would write a ticket that says roughly..."
  • If you get stuck at any step, that's useful information - tell Chiron exactly where, rather than skipping ahead and hoping it resolves itself.

Deliverable

Bring to Chiron, in one sitting or across a short series of messages:

  • Your ambiguity diagnosis and evidence (step 1)
  • The specific gap and how you'd close it (step 2)
  • Your answers to the three questions (step 3)
  • The actual ticket text (step 4) and sub-task breakdown (step 5)
  • Your sandbox export file after completing step 6, or a description of what the sandbox log showed
  • The cutover plan (step 7) and outcome-check sentence (step 8)
This is deliberately not graded by a rubric yet (per the current program design) - Chiron will walk through your artifacts with you, point out where the chain breaks or holds together, and tell you honestly which Track A/B units to revisit if something doesn't connect.

Before you start

Of the eight steps above, which one do you expect to be hardest for you, based on how the individual units went? Say why, before you start - then see if you were right.

Tell Chiron your prediction, then begin the walkthrough.
